Check before you trust

In this section you made the AI give a wrong answer. These show what kinds of mistakes to expect and how to check for them.

Evaluating AI Outputs

University of North Texas LibrariesShow details
  • FormatOnline guide with activities
  • LevelBeginner
  • Time10 to 15 min
  • AccessFree. No account needed.
  • LanguageEnglish

What this addsA 15-minute guide that corrects common beliefs about AI answers.

Key takeaways

  • Polished writing is not proof that an answer is correct.
  • Asking again gives you a different answer, not necessarily a better one.
  • Some AI tools search the web and others do not, so check which one you are using.
  • Six questions to ask: is it accurate, who is the authority, what is the evidence, is it biased, is it relevant, and what is verified versus guessed?

Some library links on the site are for UNT students only. The guide itself is open.

AI and Information Literacy

University of Maryland LibrariesShow details
  • FormatOnline guide with short videos
  • LevelBeginner to intermediate
  • TimeAbout 15 min for the fact-checking page; 1 to 2 hours for the full guide
  • AccessFree. No account needed. Licensed CC BY-NC 4.0.
  • LanguageEnglish

What this addsA step-by-step method, called lateral reading, for checking any AI answer, and how to spot bias.

Key takeaways

  • Break the answer into separate claims, then check each one in other reliable sources.
  • If the AI names a source, open it and confirm it exists and says what the AI claims.
  • Ask what your question assumed and what the AI assumed.
  • AI can leave out whole viewpoints. Asked for art history, it often gives only European art.

Keep your information safe

The course asks you never to type personal or confidential details into an AI tool. These explain why.

Be Careful What You Tell Your AI Chatbot

Stanford Institute for Human-Centered AI (HAI)Show details
  • FormatNews article
  • LevelBeginner to intermediate
  • TimeAbout 8 min
  • AccessFree. No account needed.
  • LanguageEnglish

What this addsCompares what six major AI companies do with your chats.

Key takeaways

  • All six companies studied use people's chats to train their AI by default.
  • Some keep chats indefinitely, and some let people read them.
  • Files you upload can be collected too.
  • Even a harmless question, such as asking for heart-healthy recipes, can reveal something sensitive about you. Turn off training on your chats where you can.

Published October 2025 and focused on US companies. Company policies may have changed since.

Open the article(opens in a new tab)

Safe and Secure Use of Generative AI for Individuals

Cyber Security Agency of Singapore and IMDA (government advisory)Show details
  • FormatAdvisory (2-page PDF)
  • LevelBeginner
  • TimeAbout 10 min
  • AccessFree. No account needed.
  • LanguageEnglish

What this addsPractical steps for what to keep out of AI tools, and how to spot risky AI apps.

Key takeaways

  • Even casual chats can reveal a lot about you over time. If an AI tool is hacked, that information could be used for scams.
  • Keep out your full name, address, ID numbers, and financial or medical records. Never enter workplace data without permission.
  • Be careful with unofficial AI apps and browser extensions, especially ones that ask for camera or location access they do not need.
  • Use AI to support your thinking, not replace it, and as a tool rather than a replacement for real relationships.

Written for Singapore. Where it mentions the NRIC (national ID card), think of your Aadhaar number.

Why language models hallucinate

OpenAIShow details
  • FormatBlog article
  • LevelIntermediate
  • TimeAbout 10 min
  • AccessFree. No account needed.
  • LanguageEnglish

What this addsExplains why AI makes things up, using a comparison every student knows.

Key takeaways

  • Like a student guessing on a hard exam question, AI guesses when it is unsure.
  • This happens because training and testing reward a lucky guess more than an honest "I don't know".
  • During training the model sees only fluent text, with no examples marked as wrong, so false statements can sound just as natural as true ones.
  • AI can be built to admit uncertainty. Newer models make fewer confident errors, but the problem is not fully solved.

Published September 2025. Read the blog article; the research paper linked inside it is highly technical.

Tracing the thoughts of a large language model

Anthropic (research)Show details
  • FormatResearch article with video
  • LevelAdvanced
  • TimeAbout 15 min
  • AccessFree. No account needed.
  • LanguageEnglish

What this addsWhat researchers see when they look inside an AI model while it works.

Key takeaways

  • The AI sometimes uses a shared space of ideas across languages, before answering in a particular language.
  • Although it writes one word at a time, it can plan ahead, for example choosing a rhyming word before writing a line of a poem.
  • When given a wrong hint, it sometimes builds a convincing-sounding argument to agree with the user.
  • The explanation an AI gives for its answer may not match how it actually reached that answer.
